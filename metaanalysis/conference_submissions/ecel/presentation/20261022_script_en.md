# Introduction

## ▶ S1 | Title

Thank you for the introduction. / My name is Eisuke Tokiwa, from SUNBLAZE in Japan. [pause]

Before I start, / my English is not very good, / so my talk may not be smooth. / Thank you for your patience. [pause]

If there is not enough time for questions, / please send me an email. / My email address is on the last slide. [pause]

My talk is "Modality matters for Extraversion." / It is about online learning and personality. [pause]

---

## ▶ S2 | About me

I studied graphic design at university. / Since 2024, I have done research in **personality psychology**. [pause]

I have published three papers. [pause]

**The first paper** is about **online learning and personality** / in Japanese students. / It is in Frontiers in Psychology, 2025. [pause]

**The second paper** is about **personality types**.  / It is in IEEE Access, 2026. [pause]

**The third paper** is about **workplace harassment and dark personality traits**. / It is in Frontiers in Psychology, 2026. [pause]

Today's talk **started from the first paper**. [pause]

When I wrote it, / I had one question. / **Is the result for Japan the same in other countries?** [pause]

So I started a review. / I collected many studies on online learning. / Today, I will show you **a pattern I found in this review**. [pause]

---

## ▶ S3 | Three messages

I have three messages today. [pause]

**One.** / **Conscientiousness is important in online learning, too.** [pause]

**Two.** / **Extraversion is not zero.** / **It can be plus or minus. / It depends on the course format.** [pause]

**Three.** / **The course format is not just background.** / It changes which personality helps students learn. [pause]

---

## ▶ S4 | Key words: the Big Five and non-cognitive skills

First, let me explain some words. [pause]

The first one is the **Big Five**. / It describes personality with **five traits**. [pause]

**Openness.** / Curiosity about new things. [pause]

**Conscientiousness.** / Being careful, planning, and controlling yourself. [pause]

**Extraversion.** / Liking to talk with people, / and having a lot of energy. [pause]

**Agreeableness.** / Being kind and helpful to others. [pause]

**Neuroticism.** / Feeling worried or anxious easily. [pause]

In education, / these five traits are closely linked to **non-cognitive skills** / and **social and emotional skills**. [pause]

For example, the OECD has a survey of social and emotional skills. / It is **based on the Big Five**. [pause]

So, research on personality / is also research on **skills that schools want to grow**. [pause]

Today, the two main traits are / **Conscientiousness and Extraversion**. [pause]

---

## ▶ S5 | Why personality matters in education

Why do we study personality in education? / There are three reasons. [pause]

**One. Personality is not only from our genes.** / Twin studies show that genes explain **about 40 percent**. [pause]

**Two. Personality can change.** / Studies on personality programs show clear changes / in a few months. [pause]

**Three. But schools have little time to train personality.** / So personality can be **a hint to choose the right way to learn for each student**. / In other words, we can use it for **personalized learning**. [pause]

Today's talk is about the third point. / **Which course format fits which students?** [pause]

---

# Background

## ▶ S6 | What we know about face-to-face learning

First, what do we know about face-to-face learning? [pause]

Many studies, for example Poropat 2009 and Vedel 2014, say the same thing. [pause]

**Conscientiousness has the strongest link with grades.** / The correlation is about 0.20 to 0.27. / In education research, **0.2 is a meaningful size**. [pause]

So, **what about online learning?** [pause]

---

## ▶ S7 | Why I focused on Extraversion

In online learning, / I was most interested in **Extraversion**. / I had two reasons. [pause]

**Reason one.** / In online learning, **students meet people less often**. [pause]

Of the five traits, / **Extraversion is the most connected to people**. [pause]

So I thought, / **if anything changes online, Extraversion will change the most.** [pause]

**Reason two.** / My first paper, the study of Japanese students. [pause]

In that study, **the result for Extraversion was not clear**. / One part of Extraversion, **Assertiveness**, / had a plus link with doing online tests. / But another part, **Sociability**, / went a little in the minus direction. [pause]

**Inside Extraversion, the directions were split.** [pause]

So I made a hypothesis. [pause]

**In online learning, / the link between Extraversion and grades / will be different from face-to-face learning.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S8 | My review: Extraversion was "zero"

I collected **25 studies**. / I could get numbers from **12 of them**. [pause]

**Conscientiousness was r = 0.167.** / Like face-to-face learning, it was the strongest plus link. [pause]

Openness, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism were small. / **So far, almost the same as face-to-face learning.** [pause]

And then, **Extraversion**. [pause]

**r = 0.002.** / Not plus. Not minus. / **Almost zero.** [pause]

I expected Extraversion to change the most. / But **it looked like nothing happened**. [pause]

**Is it really zero?** / Or, **is something hiding inside?** [pause]

---

## ▶ S9 | Key word: pooled r

The numbers I just showed are called **pooled r**. [pause]

A **pooled r** is **one average** / of the r values from many studies. [pause]

But **bigger studies count more**. [pause]

---

## ▶ S10 | Three formats of online learning

Now, let me explain the **formats** of online learning. [pause]

**Number one: asynchronous.** / These are recorded classes. / Students watch them any time they like. / MOOCs are a typical example. [pause]

**Number two: synchronous.** / These are live classes. / Everyone meets on Zoom at the same time. [pause]

**Number three: mixed-online.** / This mixes recorded classes and live classes. / Many university online courses use this format. [pause]

---

## ▶ S11 | The question: did mixing the formats make it "zero"?

Then, I had an idea. [pause]

The pooled r I showed you / is **one average of all three formats together**. [pause]

What if **Extraversion is minus in asynchronous courses, / and plus in mixed-online courses?** [pause]

**Then the minus and the plus cancel out. / And the average becomes zero.** [pause]

To check this, / **we have to split the studies by format, / and calculate the average again.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S12 | Key word: moderator

This idea has a name. / It is called a **moderator**. [pause]

A moderator is **a condition that changes the strength or direction of a link**. [pause]

For example, / an umbrella keeps you dry, / but **only on rainy days**. / In this case, the weather is the moderator. [pause]

So, today's question is this. / **Is the course format a moderator / of the link between personality and grades?** [pause]

---

# Method

## ▶ S13 | Splitting the studies by format

I split the 12 studies by format. [pause]

**Asynchronous: 6 studies. / Mixed-online: 5 studies. / Synchronous: only 1 study.** [pause]

Why is there only one synchronous study? [pause]

Actually, I found **three** synchronous studies. / But two of them **did not measure grades**. / They measured **satisfaction or engagement**. / Both were studies of Zoom classes / during the 2020 lockdown. [pause]

So, **only one study** could be used for grades. / We cannot make an average from one study. / So today, **I compare asynchronous and mixed-online**. [pause]

---

## ▶ S14 | Two steps of analysis

The analysis has two steps. [pause]

**Step one. I put together the studies for each format.** [pause]

For example, / I take only the studies of "Extraversion and asynchronous." / Then I combine their r values into one. / **Bigger studies count more.** [pause]

I do this for **five traits and two formats**. / So I get **ten pooled r values**. [pause]

**Step two. I put all the data into one regression model.** [pause]

The model explains the size of each r / with three things: / **which trait**, / **which format**, / and **the combination of the two**. [pause]

The important part is **the combination**. / If the format changes how a trait works, / the combination will be significant. [pause]

**Step one and step two are different calculations.** / **If both give the same answer, we can trust the result more.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S15 | Key words: confidence interval, p value, Q between

Before the results, let me explain three more words. [pause]

A **confidence interval** is a range. / **The true value is probably inside this range.** [pause]

A **p value** is **the chance of getting this result by luck alone**. / **When p is smaller than 0.05, / we say it is probably not just luck.** [pause]

**Q between** is a test. / It measures **how far apart the two pooled r values are** — / asynchronous and mixed-online. [pause]

I did not make this test myself. / It is a **standard test**, / called the "**test for subgroup differences**." [pause]

With two groups, / **when Q between is bigger than 3.84, / p is smaller than 0.05.** [pause]

---

# Results

## ▶ S16 | Table 1: results by format

Now, the results. [pause]

**For Extraversion, Q between is 17.60. / p is smaller than 0.001.** [pause]

This means / **the link between Extraversion and grades is clearly different by format.** [pause]

For Conscientiousness, Neuroticism, and Openness, / **there is no difference by format.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S17 | Figure 2: Extraversion changes direction

This is **the most important slide today**. [pause]

In **asynchronous** courses, / Extraversion is **minus**. / r = minus 0.121. [pause]

In **mixed-online** courses, / Extraversion is **plus**. / r = plus 0.059. [pause]

**When the format changes, / the direction changes.** [pause]

This is **why** the total pooled r was 0.002. / **The minus and the plus cancelled each other out.** [pause]

The "zero" result / **was actually hiding two effects in opposite directions.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S18 | The one-model check

I also checked this with the one model from step two. [pause]

**For the combination of Extraversion and mixed-online, / p = 0.009.** [pause]

**For the test of all four combinations together, / p = 0.0085.** [pause]

**The two steps gave the same answer.** / The format is **a real moderator** for Extraversion. [pause]

---

## ▶ S19 | Conscientiousness does not change

On the other hand, / Conscientiousness looks very different. [pause]

**Asynchronous: 0.190. / Mixed-online: 0.180.** [pause]

The difference is only 0.010. / p = 0.903. / **They are almost the same.** [pause]

So the two traits **behave in very different ways**. / **Conscientiousness is stable. / Extraversion depends on the format.** [pause]

---

# Discussion

## ▶ S20 | Why? And what course designers can do

Why is Extraversion minus in asynchronous courses? [pause]

This is **only a hypothesis**. / Extraverted students **get energy from talking with people**. / When they study alone for a long time, / it may be hard for them to stay motivated. [pause]

So, what can course designers do? [pause]

**For Extraversion:** / **if your course is only asynchronous, / please add a few live sessions.** / Even a few times a week / may help extraverted students. [pause]

This is **not about changing personality. / It is about course design.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S21 | Limitations

There are three limitations. [pause]

**One. The number of studies is small.** / For Extraversion, **only three studies for asynchronous, / and five for mixed-online**. / So today's result is **a discovery, not a final answer**. [pause]

**Two. The format decisions.** / I decided the format for four papers myself. / Another person might decide differently. [pause]

**Three. Only one synchronous study.** / I cannot say anything about live-only classes. [pause]

---

## ▶ S22 | Three take-home messages

Let me go back to my three messages. [pause]

**One.** / **Conscientiousness is important in online learning, too.** / It does not change with the format. [pause]

**Two.** / **Extraversion is not zero.** / **It is minus in asynchronous courses, / and plus in mixed-online courses.** / The total average was hiding this. [pause]

**Three.** / **The course format is an important moderator.** / In the future, online learning studies / **should report results for each format separately.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S23 | Thank you

Thank you very much for listening. [pause]

I am happy to take your questions. / If my English is not clear, / please tell me. [pause]

## 想定質問と回答

**Q1. Isn't the format coding subjective?（形式の判断は主観的では？）**

Yes, that is a risk. / For four papers, I decided the format / from how each paper described the class. / For example, if a paper says "no live classes," / I coded it as asynchronous. / I recorded every reason. / Next time, I want another person to check them, too.

**Q2. Does region matter — for example, Asia and the West?（地域は関係する？）**

Good question. / Some studies show that Extraversion is often minus in Asia. / But I had too few studies / to split by region and format at the same time. / I will talk about region at another conference, ICEEL 2026.

**Q3. Can we use this to select or group students?（学生の選別に使える？）**

No, I don't recommend that. / The effect is small. / It is not a tool to judge each student. / The right use is for **course design**.

**Q4. How did you calculate Q between?（深掘りされた場合）**

I used the standard test for subgroup differences. / I compare the pooled values of the two groups. / Each value is weighted by how certain it is. / The test follows a chi-square distribution / with one degree of freedom.

**Q5. Is the regression model a full meta-regression?（深掘りされた場合）**

It is a simple version of meta-regression. / I fixed the between-study variance / at the middle value of the five traits. / So I call it exploratory in the paper. / But it gives the same answer as step one.
