## Paper Title
Cultural Dimensions in Online Learning: A Hofstede-Moderated Within-Asia Synthesis of the Big Five and Academic Achievement, with a Focused Look at the Japanese Context

## All Authors
Eisuke Tokiwa

## Paper Abstract
Background. The parent meta-analysis (Research Square preprint, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-9513298/v1) reports an Extraversion × Region moderator in Big Five – academic-achievement research on online learning but does not decompose the Asia bin into its country-level constituents. Re-analysed here with a Knapp–Hartung meta-regression, the contrast is Asian pooled r = −0.095 (k = 2) vs non-Asian r = +0.050 (k = 7), F(1, 7) = 12.94, p = .009. Objective. To (i) describe how the Asian primary-pool studies differ on Hofstede 6-D country scores, and (ii) explore whether the observed Big Five – outcome directions in Japan-based studies correspond to the directions suggested by country-level trait – culture associations (Hofstede & McCrae 2004). Methods. The Asian primary pool comprises k = 2 studies with extractable Pearson r (Yu 2021, China; Rivers 2021, Japan); a third Japan-based study (Tokiwa 2025, K-12) contributes descriptive Spearman ρ. Random-effects pools use REML + HKSJ. Because residual df = 0, fits on Hofstede scores are reported only as descriptive two-country contrasts, not as meta-regression evidence. Country-level correlations from Hofstede & McCrae (2004) serve as an exploratory reference point, and engagement outcomes (Tokiwa, Spearman ρ) are kept separate from achievement outcomes (Rivers, Pearson r). Results. The observed signs correspond to the country-level reference for 3 of 5 traits (Conscientiousness+, Extraversion−, Neuroticism+) and differ for Agreeableness and Openness. Neuroticism is positive in direction in both Japan studies (engagement in one, course grade in the other; neither correlation is statistically significant). This contrasts with the null-to-negative pattern in earlier meta-analyses, including their Asian samples (Poropat 2009; Mammadov 2022), and is in line with Japan's very high Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI = 92) and Masculinity (MAS = 95). Because country-level associations do not imply individual-level effects, and the two outcomes differ in construct and metric, this correspondence is a hypothesis rather than replicated evidence about achievement. Conclusion. Treating Asia as one moderator level hides country-level differences that the current evidence cannot resolve. With k = 2, this is an exploratory methodological and narrative synthesis: it flags within-Asia heterogeneity, the gap between culture-level and individual-level data, and outcome and instrument confounds (60-item BFI-2-J vs 10-item TIPI-J) as prerequisites for future Japan-specific syntheses.
