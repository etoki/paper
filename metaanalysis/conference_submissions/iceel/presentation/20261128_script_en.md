# Introduction

## ▶ S1 ｜ Title

Thank you for the introduction. / My name is Eisuke Tokiwa, / from SUNBLAZE in Tokyo. [pause]

First, / my English is not very good. / So my talk may be a little slow. / Thank you for your patience. [pause]

If we do not have enough time for questions, / please send me an email. / My email address is on the last slide. [pause]

Today, / I will talk about **culture, personality, and online learning.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S2 ｜ About me

I have studied **personality psychology** since 2024. / I mainly study **online learning of Japanese students.** [pause]

Today's talk comes from a **review** / that I am working on. / It is a question about **culture.** [pause]

---

# Background

## ▶ S3 ｜ [Term] The Big Five

The **Big Five** is a model of personality. / It describes people with **five traits.** [pause]

**Openness** — / interest in new things. / **Conscientiousness** — / being careful and well-planned. / **Extraversion** — / being outgoing. / **Agreeableness** — / being kind and cooperative. / And **Neuroticism** — / how easily you feel anxious or worried. [pause]

In face-to-face classes, / **Conscientiousness has the strongest link with grades.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S4 ｜ A difference in "Asia"

I collected studies / on personality and grades / in online learning. [pause]

Then I split the studies / into **Asia** / and **outside Asia.** [pause]

I found a difference / in **Extraversion.** [pause]

Outside Asia, / the correlation was **plus 0.05.** / In Asia, / it was **minus 0.095.** / This difference was statistically significant. / The p value was 0.009. [pause]

So, **in Asia, / more extraverted students / had slightly lower grades.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S5 ｜ But "Asia" is only two studies

But when I looked inside, / I found a problem. [pause]

The "Asia" result came from / **only two studies.** [pause]

**One is from China** — / the study by Yu. / **The other is from Japan** — / the study by Rivers. [pause]

And **China and Japan have quite different cultures.** [pause]

Can we really put them together / and call them "Asia"? [pause]

---

## ▶ S6 ｜ [Term] Hofstede's cultural dimensions

When we compare cultures, / we often use **Hofstede's cultural dimensions.** [pause]

They give each country a score / on **six dimensions.** / Today, I will show only three. [pause]

**Individualism.** / Does the culture value the individual, / or the group? [pause]

**Uncertainty Avoidance.** / How much do people want to avoid / unclear or unknown situations? [pause]

**Masculinity.** / How much does the culture value / competition and success? [pause]

**Japan scores 92 on Uncertainty Avoidance, / and 95 on Masculinity.** / Both are very high in the world. [pause]

China scores 30 on Uncertainty Avoidance. / **That is a 62-point difference.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S7 ｜ Research questions

So, / I have two questions today. [pause]

**First.** / When we split "Asia" by country, / **can cultural differences explain the results?** [pause]

**Second.** / In Japanese studies, / **do the links between personality and learning / go in the direction that Japanese culture suggests?** [pause]

---

# Method

## ▶ S8 ｜ Method

**Step one.** / For the two studies, from China and Japan, / I put the culture scores / next to the personality–grade correlations. [pause]

But **with only two studies, / we cannot run a statistical test.** / So **this is a descriptive comparison only.** [pause]

**Step two.** / I compared the two studies from Japan. / One is the Rivers study, / with university students. / The other is my own study from 2025, / with high school students. [pause]

Then I compared the results / with a study by **Hofstede and McCrae, from 2004.** / They looked at **33 countries.** / They linked each country's culture scores / with the country's average personality. [pause]

---

# Results

## ▶ S9 ｜ Result 1: Extraversion goes the opposite way

First, / China and Japan. [pause]

The **correlation between Extraversion and grades** / was **minus 0.074 in China, / and minus 0.173 in Japan.** [pause]

Japan has **higher Individualism** than China. / 62 versus 43. [pause]

Usually, we think / **Extraversion helps more / in individualistic cultures.** / But the result was the **opposite.** / In Japan, the more individualistic country, / the Extraversion correlation was more negative. [pause]

But this is **only a comparison of two countries.** / Age and test differences / may also play a role. [pause]

---

## ▶ S10 ｜ Result 2: The two Japanese studies are very different

Next, / the two studies from Japan. [pause]

Both used **asynchronous** online learning. / But everything else was quite different. [pause]

Rivers studied **university students.** / My study looked at **high school students.** [pause]

Rivers measured **grades.** / I measured **how many videos students watched, / and how many tests they finished.** / In other words, **learning behavior.** [pause]

And the personality tests. / My study used **60 questions.** / Rivers used **10 questions.** / That is **six times different.** [pause]

With fewer questions, / there is more measurement error. / **This difference alone / could change the results.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S11 ｜ Result 3: Three of five traits match

Finally, / I compared the results / with the directions that culture suggests. [pause]

**Three of the five traits / went in the same direction:** / Conscientiousness, Extraversion, and Neuroticism. [pause]

Agreeableness and Openness / went in a different direction. [pause]

The most interesting one / is **Neuroticism.** [pause]

In both Japanese studies, / it was **positive.** / **0.17** in my study, / and **0.107** in the Rivers study. [pause]

In large earlier reviews, / the link between Neuroticism and grades / is **zero or negative.** / Even in Asian data, / it is **minus 0.19.** [pause]

So, **in Japan, / we saw the opposite direction.** [pause]

---

# Discussion

## ▶ S12 ｜ Why is Neuroticism positive? (A hypothesis)

One possible reason / is Japan's **high Uncertainty Avoidance.** [pause]

In 1986, Hofstede wrote this. / In cultures with high Uncertainty Avoidance, / **students like structured learning, / and they are rewarded for accurate answers.** [pause]

In this kind of environment, / **students who worry a little / may study more carefully and seriously.** [pause]

But this is only a **hypothesis.** [pause]

Both values are small. / **They are not statistically significant.** [pause]

Also, / the culture scores are **country-level data.** / But the correlations are **data from individual students / within one country.** / **A link between countries / may not hold for individuals.** [pause]

---

# Summary

## ▶ S13 ｜ Summary

I have three messages today. [pause]

**One.** / **"Asia" is not one thing.** / But when we split it by country, / there are **only two studies.** [pause]

**Two.** / In Japan, / **Neuroticism went in a positive direction.** / It may be related to culture. / But it is still a hypothesis. [pause]

**Three.** / Today's results are not answers. / They are **questions for future research.** / Future studies need **three things.** / **The same personality test.** / **The same grade measure.** / And **each student's own cultural values,** / measured together. [pause]

When we think about personality-based learning online, / **we should not treat national cultures as one group.** [pause]

---

## ▶ S14 ｜ Thank you

Thank you very much for listening. [pause]

If I do not catch your question, / I may ask you to say it again slowly. / Questions by email are also very welcome. [pause]

## 質疑用フレーズ（短く答える）

**聞き返す**
- "Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?"
- "Do you mean ...?"

**よくありそうな質問**

1. **Why only two studies? / What about Korea or Taiwan?**
   "In our review, / only these two Asian studies / gave a usable correlation with grades. / I did not find usable studies from Korea or Taiwan. / That is an important next step."

2. **Why did you not run a regression on the culture scores?**
   "With two studies, / the line always passes through both points. / So there is no test. / I show it only as a description."

3. **Is the Hofstede model still valid?**
   "It has criticisms. / For example, McSweeney in 2002. / But Uncertainty Avoidance was replicated / in European samples in 2014. / I use the scores only as country-level indicators."

4. **Country level and individual level — isn't that a problem?**
   "Yes. / That is the main limitation. / Country-level links may not hold for individuals. / So future studies should measure / each student's cultural values."

5. **Why is Neuroticism high in Japan?**
   "In a 56-country study by Schmitt and colleagues, / Japan had the highest average Neuroticism. / But a high average does not mean / a positive link with grades. / That is why I call it a hypothesis."

6. **Does the Big Five fit Japanese culture?**
   "It may not fully capture / culture-specific personality. / Cheung and colleagues discussed this in 2003. / I list it as a limitation."
